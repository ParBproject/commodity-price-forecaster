# Commodity price forecaster

Weekly forecasts for energy, metals, and grain futures. Prices are Yahoo Finance daily closes. The comparison is last-value, drift, and seasonal naive against ARIMA(1, 1, 1) and Prophet, scored one step ahead on a rolling origin. The Streamlit app also fits an LSTM and can show weather, a decomposition, and a simple risk table.

[Live demo](https://parbproject.github.io/commodity-price-forecaster/) reads `results/leaderboard.json`. The `gh-pages` branch is built from `site/` plus that file.

MASE is scaled by the in-sample mean absolute weekly change before the first origin. ### Do the intervals cover?

Same 52 origins, scored on the ARIMA(1, 1, 1) prediction intervals rather than the point forecast. A calibrated 95% interval should contain the close about 95% of the time. The p-value is an exact two-sided binomial test of that rate. `scripts/run_calibration.py` writes `results/calibration.json`.

On this window the intervals are too narrow for energy and precious metals (gold's nominal 95% interval held the price 44.2% of the time) and about right for the grains. The model estimates its error variance from the whole history back to 2016. When the price level and volatility rise, as gold and silver did in 2025–26, the band does not widen enough. Treat the bands as what the model implies, not as calibrated risk ranges.

### USDA yields as a regressor

Grains only. The extra feature is the log ratio of the latest USDA NASS national yield (monthly August–November Crop Production forecasts, then the January final) to the previous crop year's final yield. Release dates are taken as the 12th of the month, and the final as 12 January of the next year, so a report is not used before that date. Week t is forecast with the value known at week t−1. `scripts/run_usda_variant.py` writes `results/usda_variant.json`.

The yield feature barely moves one-week errors. Wheat improves a little; the average does not beat drift on these three grains (drift averages 0.832). Most of the report is already in the price by the weekly close. The variant stays out of the main leaderboard.

A NASS key is only needed to refresh the cache. Register at <https://quickstats.nass.usda.gov/api/> and store it as the Actions secret `USDA_NASS_API_KEY`. The `refresh-usda` workflow downloads `data/usda_cache.json` as an artifact, which is then committed. Locally: `USDA_NASS_API_KEY=... python scripts/refresh_usda.py`. The app, tests, and benchmark read the committed file and do not call the API.

## Dashboard

`app.py` has eight tabs. Default dates are 2020-01-01 through 2024-12-31.

- **Market overview.** Price, total return, annualised volatility of weekly returns, and the high and low of the last 52 weeks (the full sample if the history is shorter).
- **Forecast.** Auto-ARIMA when `pmdarima` is installed, otherwise ARIMA(1, 1, 1). Prophet, the average of those two paths, or an LSTM. The errors next to the chart are one multi-step forecast from 85% of the sample. The plotted path is a refit on the whole sample and does not change that score. Supply, demand, and weather sliders scale the plotted path only.
- **Forecast validation.** One-step last-value, drift, and seasonal naive at every remaining week. This is not the nine-commodity leaderboard above.
- **Weather.** Open-Meteo history for a chosen region, joined to weekly returns on the same dates. If the API is down, the tab draws a seeded simulated series and labels it as simulated. That series is not a model input.
- **Decomposition.** STL, with Hyndman seasonal and trend strength.
- **Risk.** Volatility, drawdown, and trend scores from the last 52 weeks. They are the same for every producer of that commodity. The geopolitical column is a fixed prior (a hash of the name plus a commodity base), not an estimated country risk.
- **Backtest.** Walk-forward ARIMA(1, 1, 1) with a chosen horizon and step.
- **Correlations.** Weekly returns for extra commodities selected in the sidebar.

Screenshots in `assets/screenshots/` are that default WTI view. The files named `01_` through `07_` predate the Backtest and Correlations tabs; the later shots include them. They show the interface, not a second published score.

Softs (coffee `KC=F`, cocoa `CC=F`, sugar `SB=F`, cotton `CT=F`) are in the dashboard only. The frozen leaderboard is the nine commodities in the snapshot.

## Tests and CI

GitHub Actions lints, compiles, and runs pytest on Python 3.10 and 3.12. The suite checks metric edge cases, the rolling-origin chronology, that each score uses only its training prefix, the trailing 52-week high and low, lagged weather in `create_features`, Hyndman seasonal and trend strength, and the ARIMA holdout being scored before the full-sample refit. It also pins the leaderboard headline numbers, recomputes the baselines and ARIMA from the snapshot, recomputes Prophet for WTI when Prophet is installed, and checks that the Results block above matches `results/leaderboard.json`. Calibration coverage for WTI and the wheat USDA variant are recomputed the same way.

## Limits

- Continuous futures from Yahoo include contract rolls.
- A relationship that held in the sample can fail in another regime.
- Interval width depends on the model. The calibration table shows where it fails.
- Weather is context for the chart. It is not in the leaderboard models.
- The scenario sliders are multipliers, not estimated probabilities.
- Tuning against one fixed holdout will overfit that holdout.
- Nothing here is a trading or hedging recommendation.
